## 1. Contractual Text Background: Section 6 of the *Additional Terms of Service* vs. the Website *Official FAQ Page*

### 1.1 Binding Legal Contract Text: Section 6 of the *Google Antigravity Additional Terms of Service* (Unmodified Since September 2026)
According to the verbatim text of the [Google Antigravity Additional Terms of Service](https://antigravity.google/terms), the legally binding contract ("Agreement") consists exclusively of three documents: (1) the *Google Terms of Service*, (2) the *Google Antigravity Additional Terms of Service*, and (3) the *Google Privacy Policy* (Note: **the website FAQ page is not included among the legally binding contractual documents enumerated in Sections 1 and 2 of the Agreement**)[^14].
The full verbatim text of Section 6 reads as follows:
> *"6. You must not abuse, harm, interfere with, or disrupt the Service. This includes, but is not limited to, using the Service in connection with products not provided by us. Using third party software, tools, or services to access the Service (e.g. using OpenClaw with Antigravity OAuth) is a breach of this Agreement. Such actions may be grounds for suspension or termination of your Antigravity and/or Gemini CLI accounts."*[^14]
> *(Plain-English Legal Scope Note: Section 6 prohibits abusing, harming, interfering with, or disrupting the Service, including using the Service in connection with non-Google products or using third-party software, tools, or services to access the Service—explicitly illustrated by extracting or pairing Antigravity OAuth credentials with third-party harnesses such as OpenClaw—under penalty of account suspension or termination.)*

### 1.2 The *Official FAQ* Entry Silently Added in Mid-October
Because the official `agy` CLI team specifically engineered and documented a headless execution mode (`agy -p "<prompt>"`, `--output-format json`, `--output-format stream-json`), its explicit architectural purpose was to allow external processes to invoke `agy` programmatically via standard input/output (`stdio`). However, around **October 6 to October 8, 2026**, without modifying a single word of Section 6 above and without sending any contractual amendment notification emails to paying subscribers, Google silently appended the following Q&A entry to the bottom of its non-contractual [Official FAQ page](https://antigravity.google/docs/faq/)[^13][^36]:
> *"## Can I use Antigravity CLI headless mode in third-party coding agents or automate account switching?*
> *Antigravity CLI headless mode (`agy -p`) is intended for direct scripting and CI workflows. Wrapping `agy` inside third-party coding agents or applications, or automating account rotation, is not supported under consumer plans."*[^13]

## 3. Phase II (2026-10-08 17:34 UTC to 2026-11-02): The October 8 Policy Reversal, FAQ Update, and Expanded Restrictions

### 3.1 The Turning Point: Reversal Within 26.5 Hours (`#186246` → `#187241`)
A cross-examination of the raw UTC timestamps on the forum reveals a striking sequence of events:
- **2026-10-07 15:03:46 UTC**: Google staff member `@chunduriv` stated in black and white in **#186246**: *"From a policy standpoint, there is no difference whether the unmodified `agy` subprocess is started by your own script, a local coordinator, or another local coding assistant (such as Claude Code)."*[^32]
- **2026-10-08 16:23:34 UTC**: `@Ambati_Rajendra` was still reassuring a user in **#187536** that combining `agy` with Codex and Claude Code for automated task handoffs inside a personal orchestrator was completely permissible (*"Yes... Does section 6 apply? No"*)[^33].
- **2026-10-08 17:34:41 UTC** (**just 71 minutes after `#187536`, and 26 hours and 31 minutes after `@chunduriv`'s own reply in `#186246`**): Responding in thread **#187241** to annual Google AI Pro subscriber `@ALAMDAR_BALOCH`—who asked the exact same question about invoking the official `agy` CLI from Codex—`@chunduriv` suddenly prefaced his post with a legal disclaimer and issued a diametrically opposed account suspension warning[^34]:
  > *"While we cannot provide formal legal interpretations of the Terms of Service... While `agy` offers a documented headless mode (`agy -p`) for your own local scripts and CI pipelines, **we do not support using third-party coding agents (like Codex, Claude Code, OpenClaw, or OpenCode) to orchestrate or wrap `agy` under a consumer login. Routing third-party agent prompts or automated loops through Antigravity can trigger automated ToS enforcement and account suspension.**"*[^34]

### 3.2 Declaring That "The FAQ Supersedes Earlier Forum Replies" and Direct Confrontation by Paying Subscribers (`#189905` & `#191689`)
1. **2026-10-16 (`#189905` Formally Repudiates Prior Official Replies)**:
   After discovering that the website FAQ directly contradicted official staff responses in threads such as `#185992`, developer `@Amane2418` opened a thread requesting an unambiguous Yes/No clarification. `@Rebbanpalli_Naveen`—the exact same engineer who 11 days earlier in `#186952` had personally assured `@Hossy` that invoking `agy.exe` from Codex was *"completely fine to use"*—stepped in to state:
   > *"Current Official Guidance: **The Antigravity FAQ reflects our current policy for Antigravity CLI headless mode and supersedes earlier forum comments where they differ.** ... Even if you invoke the official, unmodified `agy` binary as a local subprocess without extracting OAuth tokens or reverse-engineering internal endpoints, wrapping `agy` inside third-party coding agents or applications—or automating account rotation—is not supported under consumer plans... violates our Terms of Service and can result in account suspension or termination."*[^35]
2. **2026-10-23 to 10-27 (`#191689` Subscriber Cross-Examination & Official Admission That the ToS Was Never Modified)**:
   In thread `#191689`, paying Google AI Pro subscriber `@eliavb` pointed out that both Anthropic (Claude) and OpenAI permit individual subscribers to use their subscription quotas in custom local environments via their official CLIs (Claude Code and Codex), whereas Google alone demands that users pay both a monthly subscription fee and separate per-token API fees[^36]. Subsequently, users `@b_w` (Posts #4 and #6) and `@Tora39` (Post #7) directly cited the links to `#186952` (Oct 5) and `#186246` (Oct 7) to challenge `@Rebbanpalli_Naveen`:

   - In Post #5 (Oct 26) and Post #8 (Oct 27), `@Rebbanpalli_Naveen` was forced to publicly concede:
     > *"To clarify the timeline for `@b_w` and answer `@Tora39`’s question (a): **the Google Antigravity Terms of Service themselves were not modified after September; rather, the Official FAQ was updated in mid-October 2026 to add the section ‘Can I use Antigravity CLI headless mode in third-party coding agents or automate account switching?’** ... Earlier forum replies in #186246 and #186952 gave an overly broad interpretation before that FAQ entry was published."*[^36]

### 3.3 Escalating Restrictions: Banning Even "Local File Task Handoffs" and "Standalone Python Coordinators" (`#190815` / `#196472` / `#190756`)
In subsequent late-October responses, Google staff expanded the prohibition far beyond direct child-process `stdio` piping to encompass virtually every local workflow involving external AI coordination:
1. **Banning Task Handoffs Between Claude Code and a Standalone Python Script via Local Folders (`#190815`, 2026-10-28)**:
   User `@Lars` proposed an architecture where a standalone Python script reads task files from a local folder and invokes `agy -p`, while Claude Code never touches the `agy` process or Google credentials and merely prepares task files in the local directory. `@Ambati_Rajendra` flatly rejected the setup:
   > *"**That still counts as third-party agent coordination. Passing tasks from Claude Code to `agy -p` through local files instead of stdin/stdout pipes still uses consumer quota as a backend worker for an external coding agent**, so it requires a Gemini API key or Gemini Enterprise."*[^38]
2. **Banning Invocation of `agy -p` via an Independent Local Python Coordination Service (`#196472`, 2026-10-29)**:
   User `@Dsssyc` asked whether having an independent local Python task coordination service (rather than the host agent directly) schedule and launch the unmodified `agy -p` process was compliant. `@Rebbanpalli_Naveen` likewise rejected it, stating that whenever `agy` under a personal login serves as an execution backend for an external multi-agent framework, it can trigger automated account suspension[^40]. When `@Dsssyc` followed up in Post #3 asking whether it would still be a violation if the framework merely generated a script that the human user manually copied and pasted into a terminal to run before passing the output back to the framework for review, official staff had failed to provide a coherent answer as of November 4[^40].
3. **Banning Unattended Automatic Triggering by Personal Monitoring Apps (`#190756`, 2026-10-28)**:
   User `@xorz` asked whether a custom private monitoring utility on Windows was allowed to automatically invoke `agy -p` at low frequency to analyze logs upon detecting system errors. `@Ambati_Rajendra` replied that while running scripts manually is allowed, **any unattended or automatic background requests (such as event-driven daemons or polling loops) are unsupported under consumer plans and may trigger automated abuse/ToS suspensions even with strict local rate limiting**[^39].

## 4. Technical Detection Limitations, Protocol Differences, and the Two-Strike Appeal Process

### 4.1 OS-Level Detection Limitations and the `agy acp` vs. `agy -p` Distinction (`@oli_hlmpn`'s Critique)
In forum threads **#190756** (Oct 29) and **#197131** (Nov 2), developers `@oli_hlmpn` and `@Jay10` (`#189905`) highlighted three contradictions in Google's current policy from both computer science and user experience standpoints[^35][^39][^41]:
1. **Indistinguishability of Process Trees and Text Provenance ("Dice-Roll Suspensions")**:
   At the Linux/Windows operating system level, when `agy -p "<prompt>"` executes, the `agy` process can only see that its parent process is `bash`, `powershell`, or `python3`. Neither the OS kernel nor the `agy` binary has any way of knowing whether that prompt string or local `.md` task file was typed into `vim` by a human five minutes earlier or generated with the assistance of Claude Code or ChatGPT[^37][^39]. As `@oli_hlmpn` observed:
   > *"And lastly, the most concerning part, **since determining if the command was executed by a local go server, a python script, or a 3 line bash script, is borderline impossible, does this not mean that even perfectly acceptable, fair use, explicitly allowed usage ends up being a dice roll?** If something that is explicitly allowed comes with the caveat of potentially causing a permanent suspension of a business account, does it not become a significant problem when that is not communicated clearly?"*[^39]
2. **Policy Difference Between `agy acp` (Permitting Third-Party Clients via `stdio`) and `agy -p` (Disallowing Third-Party Clients via `stdio`)**:
   In thread `#197131` (Nov 2, 2026) and the CLI `v1.5.0` release (Nov 4, 2026), Google explicitly permitted third-party editors and clients (such as Zed, Neovim, and T3 Code) to spawn `agy acp` (or `antigravity-acp`) over standard input/output (`stdio`) using personal subscription OAuth sign-in (`oauth-personal`)[^11][^41]. Yet on the exact same machine and under the exact same personal account, having a third-party tool spawn `agy --output-format stream-json -p` over `stdio` is classified as a Trust & Safety violation subject to account suspension[^39][^41].
3. **Lack of Client-Side Warnings (`@Jay10`)**:
   When a user configures a coding assistant to invoke `agy -p`, the command executes smoothly locally and returns output without throwing any error or displaying any interactive confirmation prompt. Even if the CLI were to print a warning, it would be captured by the parent process's `stdout` pipe and never seen by the human user—until the user's Google account receives a `403 TOS_VIOLATION` notice[^35].

### 4.2 Analysis: Why Frontline Staff Issued 10 Approvals Before Changing Position on October 8 [Analytical Inference]
*(Note: This subsection presents an analytical reconstruction based on the verified timestamps, forum Q&A records, and enterprise compliance mechanisms documented above, and is explicitly labeled as **[Analytical Inference]**.)*
1. **The Initial 10 Affirmative Replies by Frontline Engineers Aligned with Standard CLI Design and the Literal Text of Section 6**:
   From a Unix CLI design standpoint, `agy` specifically provides `-p` (`--print`) and `--output-format json / stream-json` for the sole purpose of enabling external programs to invoke it via `stdio`. Furthermore, from a security and billing architecture perspective, so long as an external program invokes the unmodified official `agy` binary without extracting OAuth tokens, 100% of authentication, telemetry reporting, and 5-hour/weekly quota deductions remain strictly governed by Google's official binary and backend (`cloudcode-pa.googleapis.com`)[^31][^32]. Consequently, the answers given by `@Ambati_Rajendra`, `@Rebbanpalli_Naveen`, and `@chunduriv` between September 16 and October 8 at 16:23 UTC were technical assessments grounded in the original text of Section 6 (which targeted credential-harvesting tools like early OpenClaw that extracted OAuth tokens to hit private endpoints directly)[^24][^31][^32].
2. **Commercial Tier Separation and Updating Policy via FAQ**:
   During the first week of October, as an increasing number of paying users in threads `#185992`, `#186246`, `#186952`, and `#187536` requested explicit confirmation on whether they could plug their personal subscriptions into Claude Code, Codex, or multi-agent orchestrators to avoid pay-as-you-go API charges—and specifically asked whether those staff replies represented Google's binding official position[^28][^31][^32][^33]—the usage pattern conflicted with product monetization boundaries. Google sought to keep fixed-price consumer subscriptions ($20 Pro / $200 Ultra) limited to direct interactive sessions while requiring multi-agent or automated workflows to use metered Google AI Studio API tokens or enterprise Vertex AI (`Gemini Enterprise`) seats[^36][^38][^40].
   Because formally amending the *Additional Terms of Service (ToS)* requires legal review and advance notice to subscribers, the policy change was instead published by **updating the *Official FAQ page* around October 8 without amending the ToS, while support staff added standardized legal disclaimers to subsequent replies and noted that their earlier answers had "given an overly broad interpretation"**[^34][^35][^36].

### 4.3 The `cloudcode-pa.googleapis.com` Suspension Mechanism and Unresolved Appeals (>100 Days)
1. **Backend Enforcement Gate and Cross-Product Collateral Lockout**:
   Antigravity's anti-abuse enforcement operates at the `cloudcode-pa.googleapis.com` backend gateway. Once an account is flagged, the client receives `HTTP 403 PERMISSION_DENIED` (`reason: TOS_VIOLATION`) and prints the following terminal error:
   `Eligibility check failed: PERMISSION_DENIED (code 403): This service has been disabled in this account for violation of Terms of Service. Please submit an appeal to continue using this product.`[^8][^10][^45]
   Because all tools share the same backend enforcement layer, a single flag **simultaneously locks out the Antigravity 2.0 desktop app, Antigravity IDE, `agy` CLI, legacy `@google/gemini-cli`, and `Gemini Code Assist` across the user's entire Google account** (additionally, some personal `@gmail.com` paying accounts have been hit with `Error #3501`, erroneously flagging them as requiring an enterprise license or `SUBSCRIPTION_REQUIRED`)[^8][^9][^45].
2. **The Two-Strike Policy and Broken Appeal Reinstatement Sync**:
   Although Google maintainer `@jackwotherspoon` promised in `Discussion #20632` that first-time offenders who complete the Google Form re-certification would have their access restored within 1–2 days via a periodic automated sync job (while second violations result in permanent bans)[^9], GitHub **Issue #927** (`@jlwainwright`, banned for over 100 days since May 25, 2026; Google One Case ID `2-9245000040533`) and **Issue #29576 / Forum #186089** (`@myessaylab1234`, who submitted the appeal form multiple times—each promising a 24–72 hour review—without ever receiving a response) prove that **the automated reinstatement sync is completely broken for many accounts. When paying subscribers contact Google One Support and Google Developer Profile Support, both teams reply that they have no visibility into or escalation path for ToS suspensions, leaving paying customers trapped in a bureaucratic dead end**[^8][^10][^45].
